How to Read an MSEDCL Solar Bill After Net Metering in Pune

Use MSEDCL’s published bill format to distinguish grid import, solar export, generation, offset, banked units and the final billed quantity.

NOV 2026 7 min read E Green Solution

Your first electricity bill after rooftop solar is commissioned can look unfamiliar. MSEDCL's published LTIP solar format separates grid import, export, solar generation and settlement fields. Reading those as distinct measurements is the key to understanding why the bill may not equal a simple “units used minus units made” calculation.

This guide focuses on interpreting an MSEDCL solar bill after meter installation. It does not cover the application or approval process; for that, see our separate MSEDCL net-metering process guide for Pune.

Quick answer: check the bill period and meter number first. Then read import units, export units, generation, offset or banked units, billed units and monetary charges as separate lines.

Import, export and generation are different

Import is electricity recorded as drawn from the grid. Export is electricity recorded as sent to the grid. Generation is output recorded by the solar-generation meter. MSEDCL's sample bill displays these in separate columns. They should not be treated as interchangeable: a solar plant can serve household loads while generating, so generation and export are not necessarily the same total.

The official bill format can show rows for time periods as well as a total. Use the period and meter information printed on your own bill, because the exact arrangement can vary with the connection and metering setup.

A practical way to read your bill

  1. Confirm the period and account. Check the consumer number, bill dates, tariff category and meter serial numbers. A bill covering only part of the period after commissioning needs to be compared with the actual activation and reading dates.
  2. Read the import section. Compare previous and current readings for each displayed tariff period. Check any import adjustment and meter factor before comparing the resulting import units.
  3. Read the export section separately. Repeat the date and reading check for export. Do not subtract a meter's cumulative display from a generation meter's display; they measure different flows.
  4. Check generation. Compare the generation meter with the inverter app for the same period. The app may use a different time window or measurement point, so a small difference alone does not establish a billing error.
  5. Follow the settlement lines. Locate any previous banked balance, offset, current banked balance and billed units. The applicable Maharashtra Electricity Regulatory Commission (MERC) rules and your approved arrangement determine how units are settled. MSEDCL's current renewable-energy regulations page is the reference point for current rules.
  6. Review the money section. Energy quantity is only one part of the payable amount. Check fixed charges, duties, other listed charges, adjustments and any prior balance against the detailed calculation on the bill.

Why “import minus export” may not match billed units

A one-line subtraction can be a useful rough check in a simple example, but it is not a safe way to audit every MSEDCL account. The published format separates time-period readings and includes adjustment, offset and banked-unit fields. The settlement method depends on the applicable regulations and the consumer's metering arrangement. Read the billed-units calculation printed on the statement and consult the current rules if a line is unclear.

Important: the sample format is an illustration of field names, not a promise of how every account will be settled. Do not use another consumer's figures or an online calculator as a substitute for your own bill and approved connection details.

If this is your first bill after solar commissioning

Write down the date the bidirectional meter was installed and the date the solar system was approved or synchronized. Compare those dates with the bill period and meter readings. If the statement covers days before the new meter was active, or a meter number does not match the installed device, ask MSEDCL or your installer to explain the billing period and readings. Keep the bill PDF, meter photographs with visible date context, inverter generation record and commissioning documents together.

A five-point mismatch checklist

  • Does the consumer number and meter serial match the premises and installed meter?
  • Do the bill's reading dates match the period you are checking?
  • Are import, export and generation shown as separate registers?
  • Are the meter factor and import/export adjustments included in the units?
  • Can you trace the billed units to the bill's offset, banked balance and applicable calculation?

If the figures still do not reconcile, contact MSEDCL through its official consumer channels with the bill month, consumer number, meter serial, relevant reading photographs and a concise description of the line in question. Avoid posting an unredacted bill publicly because it contains account and address information.

Use MSEDCL's own sample format

MSEDCL's Know Your Bill Format page lists an LTIP bill with a net meter for rooftop solar. The current published November 2026 sample solar bill shows the solar net-meter consumption table and its import, export, generation and settlement fields. Use it to recognize labels, then apply the dates and numbers on your own account.

Frequently asked questions

Does solar generation equal the export units on my MSEDCL bill?

Not necessarily. Generation measures solar output; export measures energy sent to the grid. The home's own use and the measurement points make them different quantities.

Can I calculate my solar bill by subtracting export from import?

Use subtraction only as a rough check where the applicable arrangement permits it. The MSEDCL bill may show time-period registers, adjustments, offsets and banked units, so follow the calculation printed on your statement and the current applicable rules.

Why is my first bill after installation still showing charges?

A solar connection does not necessarily remove every charge, and the bill period may include days before the meter was activated. Check the bill period, billed units and charge breakdown; contact MSEDCL or your installer if the meter or dates do not match.

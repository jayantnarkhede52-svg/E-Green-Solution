Does Solar Work During a Power Cut in Pune?

Understand what happens to an on-grid rooftop system when MSEDCL power goes off, and how to plan backup for essential appliances.

NOV 20268 min readE Green Solution
Short answer: A conventional grid-tied solar system generally stops supplying your home when the grid fails, even in bright sunshine. Solar backup requires equipment designed to isolate safely from the grid and supply selected loads. Battery capacity, inverter capability and wiring determine what stays on.

It surprises many Pune homeowners: the rooftop panels are in the sun, yet the lights go out with the neighbourhood supply. The panels are only one part of the system. A normal grid-tied inverter depends on the utility supply for its operating reference and disconnects when that supply is lost. The UPNEDA rooftop solar FAQ explains that a standard grid-connected system does not provide power during a cut.

Why does on-grid solar stop during a power cut?

The grid is a shared electrical network. When MSEDCL supply fails, an ordinary grid-tied inverter disconnects rather than continuing to energise the grid. This protective behaviour is commonly called anti-islanding. It helps avoid an unintended energised section of network while a fault is being investigated or repairs are underway.

Panels can still produce DC electricity in sunlight, but that does not mean the home can use it. The inverter and system wiring must be designed to establish a safe, separate supply. Never bypass a protective device or connect a generator, home UPS or battery to solar equipment without a qualified professional’s design.

Which solar systems can provide backup?

Standard on-grid: Usually chosen to offset grid electricity use. Without a manufacturer-supported backup arrangement it switches off during an outage. A net meter records grid exchange; it does not store electricity or create backup power.

Hybrid with battery backup: A compatible hybrid inverter, properly sized battery and backup wiring can supply designated circuits after safely isolating from the grid. Not every hybrid product supports the same backup mode, switching time, solar charging or output. Ask for the specific model’s documentation and a demonstration of the proposed operating modes.

Off-grid system: A standalone inverter and storage system can supply a separate set of loads without relying on the utility grid, subject to its design and available energy. It must be sized for the loads and the expected periods of poor sun.

Some manufacturers offer limited daytime backup outlets or other special configurations. They are model-specific and may not provide whole-house power or night-time backup. Check the written specification rather than assuming every system needs—or can work without—a battery. For equipment choices, see our hybrid solar and lithium battery guide.

How much backup does a home actually need?

Start by listing essential appliances, their running watts and the hours they should operate. Add their energy use: watts × hours ÷ 1,000 gives kilowatt-hours (kWh). For example, 200 W of combined lights, fans and a router running for four hours uses approximately 0.8 kWh at the appliances. This is an illustrative load calculation, not a battery recommendation.

The battery must deliver more stored energy than that example to account for conversion losses, usable capacity and the manufacturer’s operating limits. Also check the inverter’s continuous output and surge capability: a pump or refrigerator compressor can need more power when starting. A large battery cannot compensate for an inverter that is too small to start the load.

Ask the installer which circuits will be on the backup board. Many homes back up lighting, fans, a router and selected sockets; air conditioners, heaters, lifts or pumps may require a larger and more carefully engineered system. Runtime varies with actual loads, battery charge and condition, temperature and available sunlight.

What happens when grid power returns?

The sequence depends on the inverter, transfer equipment and its settings. A properly designed system checks the supply and returns to its permitted operating mode according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Ask the installer to demonstrate this at handover and explain whether there is a noticeable interruption. Keep the operating manual and emergency contact near the equipment. During a prolonged outage, turn off nonessential loads so that stored energy lasts longer. Never operate unfamiliar switches or alter protective settings without guidance from the installer.

Can existing solar or a home UPS be upgraded?

Sometimes, but a retrofit is not automatic. Record your existing solar inverter model, panel capacity, main distribution board and current UPS or battery details. An installer can check whether the manufacturer supports a backup configuration, whether separate compatible equipment is required, and how the protected circuits would be isolated and controlled.

Request a written scope covering equipment, wiring, protection, backup loads, monitoring and any approvals or changes to your grid connection. Do not assume an existing home UPS can accept solar generation or support the solar inverter during an outage. Utility and scheme conditions depend on the actual project; verify current requirements with MSEDCL before making changes.

Questions to ask before ordering solar backup

  1. Which exact inverter and battery models are proposed, and do their manuals support the intended backup configuration?
  2. Which circuits and appliances will remain powered, and what are the inverter’s continuous and surge limits?
  3. What usable battery energy and estimated runtime apply to my appliance list?
  4. Can the system charge from solar during an outage, and under what conditions?
  5. How does it isolate from the grid, and who will install and test the protection?
  6. What happens when the grid returns, and what warranty applies to the inverter, battery and installation?

For Pune homes, begin with a recent electricity bill, an outage history and a list of essential loads. Our residential solar team can discuss rooftop suitability and a site-specific proposal. The solar savings calculator is an initial bill-savings estimate; it does not size a backup battery.

Frequently asked questions

Will an on-grid solar system run without MSEDCL power?

A conventional on-grid system normally disconnects during a grid outage. Backup requires a separately designed and supported arrangement; confirm the exact inverter and wiring.

Will solar panels power my home at night?

Panels do not generate useful solar electricity after dark. Night-time backup needs stored energy or another appropriately designed supply.

Can one battery run the whole house?

It depends on usable battery energy, inverter output, starting loads and the selected circuits. Request a load calculation instead of choosing by panel capacity alone.

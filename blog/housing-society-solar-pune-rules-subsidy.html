Housing Society Solar in Pune: Planning & MSEDCL Guide

E Green Engineering Team • October 2026 • Verified Technical Guide
Home > Blog > Housing Society Rooftop Solar Pune

In residential neighborhoods across Pune and PCMC—from high-rises in Wakad, Hinjawadi, and Baner-Bavdhan to mature apartment complexes in Kothrud, Hadapsar, and Kalyani Nagar—the biggest financial burden draining society maintenance funds is the MSEDCL common electricity bill.

Common utilities such as passenger elevators, basement ventilation, sewage treatment plants (STP), borewell and hydro-pneumatic water booster pumps, EV charging bays, and clubhouse air conditioning operate around the clock. Under current MSEDCL commercial and high-slab residential tariff schedules, electricity rates regularly climb above ₹12 to ₹20 per unit with fuel adjustment charges (FAC) and electricity duty added.

Eligible societies can explore common-facility solar support under PM Surya Ghar. Bill savings and payback depend on meter loads, system design, generation, tariff and settlement rules; subsidy eligibility and payment require approval.

1. Legal Framework: Maharashtra Co-operative Societies (MCS) Act & Terrace Rights

One of the most frequent hurdles faced by managing committees is member hesitation regarding terrace ownership. Questions frequently arise: "Can top-floor flat owners object?" or "Do we need 100% member consensus to install solar?"

Under the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies (MCS) Act, 1960, the Model Bye-Laws (specifically Bye-Law No. 168 & 169), and the Maharashtra Apartment Ownership Act, 1970, the building terrace slab is classified as an undivided common amenity and society property. No individual member—including top-floor or penthouse occupants—holds exclusive or transferable title to open terrace space unless explicitly deeded in conveyance as private property.

What Does the Law Say on Solar Approvals?

Under guidelines issued by the Ministry of New & Renewable Energy (MNRE) and the Department of Co-operation, Government of Maharashtra:

  • No Unanimous Vote Required: Green energy initiatives for common utility bill reduction do not require 100% member sign-off.
  • Obtain society approval: Follow the society’s registered bylaws and current Maharashtra co-operative law when seeking approval for a rooftop project. Ask a qualified legal adviser about the required voting process.
  • Structural Safety Priority: The managing committee must ensure structural integrity, professional waterproofing safeguards, and maintain unrestricted access to fire exits, lift machine rooms, and overhead water storage tanks.

2. Ready-to-Use AGM Model Resolution Draft for Pune Housing Societies

To help managing committees fast-track approvals without legal delays, our legal and technical team at E Green Solution has formulated this standardized resolution format, accepted by MSEDCL Pune circles (Ganeshkhind, Rasta Peth, Pimpri):

AGENDA ITEM: INSTALLATION OF ROOFTOP SOLAR PV SYSTEM FOR COMMON UTILITIES UNDER PM SURYA GHAR GHS SCHEME

"RESOLVED THAT the approval of the General Body be and is hereby accorded to install a [Capacity in kW, e.g., 50 kWp / 100 kWp] Grid-Connected Rooftop Solar Photovoltaic (RTPV) System on the common building terrace of the Society for offsetting electricity consumption of common meters (Lifts, Water Pumps, STP, Common Area Lighting).

FURTHER RESOLVED THAT the Managing Committee is authorized to apply for the Central Government subsidy of ₹18,000 per kW under the PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana (GHS Component), execute net metering agreements with MSEDCL, and utilize available Sinking / Reserve Funds or special green contribution as sanctioned.

FURTHER RESOLVED THAT the Chairman, Secretary, and Treasurer be and are hereby jointly authorized to sign contract documents, work orders with empanelled solar EPC vendor M/s E Green Solution, submit statutory CEI electrical inspectorate filings, and take all necessary steps to commission the project."

Proposed By: [Member Name]   |   Seconded By: [Member Name]   |   Passed by: [Unanimous / Majority]

3. PM Surya Ghar for housing society common facilities

Eligible Group Housing Societies and Resident Welfare Associations can apply for ₹18,000 per kW of central financial assistance for common facilities, including EV charging. The scheme applies a 3 kW per dwelling limit and a 500 kW ceiling. Individual rooftop plants installed by residents count toward the shared ceiling.

Confirm the eligible capacity and equipment requirements before budgeting. Payment depends on scheme eligibility, commissioning and approval. See MSEDCL’s current scheme guidance and the DCR equipment guide.

4. Size the system from common-meter bills

List each common meter, collect twelve months of bills and note when lifts, pumps and lighting draw power. Match the available roof area and daytime demand to the proposed capacity. Compare quotes before subsidy and ask for the generation, tariff and self-consumption assumptions used to estimate savings.

6. Structure Engineering: Preserving 100% Terrace Living Space

A top objection during society AGMs is: "Will solar panels take away our terrace? Where will residents walk, do morning exercise, or dry clothes?"

Modern housing society engineering solves this completely. Rather than mounting panels flush against the floor tiles, E Green Solution designs elevated solar pergolas and superstructures (7 to 9.5 feet height clearance):

  • Clear Headroom: Residents can walk underneath freely, hold community get-togethers, practice yoga, or relax in shaded comfort.
  • Top-Floor Cooling: The elevated canopy acts as a protective shield against Pune's scorching summer heat, reducing ambient temperatures in top-floor penthouses by 3°C to 5°C.
  • Zero Roof Punctures: We anchor onto existing structural RCC column beam caps or deploy certified civil concrete ballasts, eliminating any risk of water seepage during monsoon downpours.
  • Wind Load Compliance: Structures are engineered to withstand cyclonic wind gusts up to 150 km/h in accordance with IS 875 standards. Read our engineering guide on elevated rooftop solar structures.

7. Step-by-Step MSEDCL Net Metering Workflow for Pune Societies

Managing committees often worry about navigating bureaucratic red tape. Here is how E Green Solution handles the entire turnkey process from paperwork to power generation:

  1. 1. Site Survey & Bill Audit: Our engineers evaluate historical MSEDCL 12-month common light bills, analyze sanction load, and map terrace shadow profiles.
  2. 2. Committee Presentation & AGM Assistance: We prepare a customized financial presentation, 25-year ROI model, and 3D architectural render to share with society members.
  3. 3. MSEDCL Feasibility Application: We file the technical feasibility request on the Mahadiscom Renewable Energy Portal under the society's common Consumer Number.
  4. 4. CEI Safety Inspection: For solar plants exceeding 10 kW, our certified liaisons coordinate mandatory safety clearance from the Chief Electrical Inspector (CEI), Government of Maharashtra.
  5. 5. Structural Fabrication & Module Installation: Hot-dip galvanized structure erection, panel installation, inverter wiring, and chemical earthing pits.
  6. 6. Bi-directional Net Meter Synchronization: MSEDCL testing laboratory certifies and commissions the bi-directional net meter.
  7. 7. Subsidy Disbursement: The commissioning record supports the claim on the PM Surya Ghar portal. Disbursement depends on approval of the eligible application.

8. Maintenance & Cleaning for Housing Society Rooftops

Rooftop solar panels in Pune accumulate particulate dust from vehicular emissions and nearby construction. Without scheduled cleaning, energy yield drops by 15% to 25%.

For housing societies, E Green Solution installs integrated high-pressure water piping networks with quick-connect nozzles along the terrace parapet. We also provide society facility teams with specialized soft-bristle nylon telescopic poles or offer comprehensive quarterly solar panel cleaning and solar AMC maintenance contracts.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Can an individual top-floor flat owner object to a society solar plant?

No. Under the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act and standard Bye-Laws, the terrace slab is undivided common property owned by the society. When the General Body passes a majority resolution to install solar for common cost reduction, individual flat owners cannot veto the project, provided waterproofing guarantees and structural safety guidelines are strictly followed.

What is the exact voting percentage needed in the AGM?

Voting requirements depend on the society’s registered bylaws and current law. Confirm the process with the society’s legal adviser before holding an AGM or SGM.

How much subsidy is given under PM Surya Ghar for housing societies?

Eligible societies may receive ₹18,000 per kW for approved common-facility capacity, within the scheme limits. Check the MSEDCL rules for the current limits and equipment requirements.
